Spain held its first general strike in 14 years on Wednesday as some four million workers — a fifth of the labour force — walked out over housing costs, three weeks before an election that could put the far right in power. Unions called it largely peaceful, but Barcelona saw burning barricades and clashes, transport was cut back and arrests were reported.

COP31's leaders' summit opened in Antalya, Türkiye, with the presidency pressing electrification — 35% of global energy use by 2035 — as the test of whether pledges become action. Erdogan and Guterres opened the session, Greta Thunberg attended for the first time since 2021, and the Global Carbon Project projected fossil-fuel emissions to dip 0.4% in 2026.

US, Russian and Ukrainian negotiators held the first peace talks in nine months in Abu Dhabi; Marco Rubio called them "very productive" but said nothing was close to settled. In Gaza, Israeli strikes killed at least eight Palestinians, including a father and his 13-year-old son, despite the US-brokered ceasefire. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two oil tankers caught fire after taking an "unauthorised route" through the Strait of Hormuz.

The US House voted 211-204 to bury an impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth; Donald Trump called it an "exoneration". October consumer prices rose 0.4%, above forecasts, and markets priced roughly 90% odds of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate rise in December.

Wall Street climbed anyway: the Nasdaq rose 1.6% to 25,466 and the S&P 500 1.31% to 7,619, while Europe closed at a record after CoreWeave jumped about 25% on strong earnings. Nvidia gained 2.9%, recovering part of a near-10% slide after Jensen Huang confirmed an affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. Anthropic set terms for an IPO next week that could value it at up to $1.4 trillion.

Hoax bomb threats emptied some 300 schools in Italy and hit schools in Britain and Ireland, in what officials suspect is a hybrid campaign.
